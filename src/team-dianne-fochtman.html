Dianne Fochtman

Co-Founder

My name is Dianne Fochtman and I grew up in Elmhurst, Illinois, a suburb of Chicago. I attended St. Mary’s School of Nursing in Rochester, MN, graduating with her RN diploma. I started my long nursing career in the Pediatric Intensive Care Unit at Children’s Memorial Hospital in Chicago. While working as a staff nurse and respiratory therapist, I returned to school part time to obtain my BSN. I decided to specialize in Pediatric Oncology Nursing and moved to California to pursue a master’s degree in nursing from UCLA. Eventually I achieved my doctorate in nursing from the University of Hawaii.

While working at Children’s Hospital, I met Frank Seleny, who was a pediatric anesthesiologist there. Frank and I married in 1976.In 1992 when Frank retired, we moved to Honolulu, where we spent many happy years before Frank’s death in 2004.

I continued my career in nursing in Honolulu, working at Kapiolani Medical Center for Women and Children as a Pediatric Nurse Practitioner for 27 years in Pediatric Oncology and later as a Palliative Care Coordinator. The final portion of my professional nursing career was spent at Hospice Hawaii (later Navian Hawaii), where I founded the first pediatric hospice in Hawaii. I continued to support that endeavor until my retirement, after 7 years in 2019.

I have been an active member of the Association of Pediatric Hematology/Oncology nurses, serving in many capacities with the organization. I founded the Journal of Pediatric Hematology/Oncology Nursing and was a lead editor on the 4 editions of the book, “Nursing Care of Children and Adolescents with Cancer and Blood Disorders”.

While in Chicago, I participated in a summer camp for children who had cancer or were survivors of childhood cancer and another summer camp for adolescents and young adults in the same situation. I have participated in the operation of these camps for many years and continue my commitment in my retirement.

Hale Anelalani is a dream that I have had throughout much of my career. Even in retirement I still pursue the hope to make life better for children with life-threatening conditions and their families.
