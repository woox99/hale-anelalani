Bereaved mother to Kiki girl

Juliana Pamplona

Co-Founder

My name is Juliana Pamplona and I am an ordinary person living an extraordinary life on Earth, one forever changed by my children, Rocco and Kiara “Kiki,” my greatest teachers in unconditional love, joy, and the sacred journey of motherhood.

On September 15, 2023, Kiki, just 10 years old, was diagnosed with a rare and aggressive brain cancer known as Diffuse Intrinsic Pontine Glioma (DIPG). After a courageous six-month battle, navigating both the limits of medicine and the depths of faith, Kiki passed on March 12, 2024, my 44th birthday.

Her passing was not an end, but a beginning, opening gates that transcend the five senses, like an eruption into spirituality. It felt as though we were all dancing between two worlds, heaven and earth.

This experience opened a profound spiritual awakening, one that blurred the lines between heaven and earth, grief and grace. Through unimaginable loss came a deeper connection to faith, surrender, and the understanding that healing does not always come in the ways we expect. In this journey, moments of synchronicity often appeared, gentle reminders of something greater at work. As Albert Einstein once said, “Coincidences are God’s way of remaining anonymous.”

During Kiki’s illness, our family faced not only the heartbreak of her condition, but also the overwhelming challenges of a medical system with limited support for children with rare and complex diseases. We fought tirelessly for treatments, equipment, and options, often hearing the words no parent should ever hear: “There’s nothing we can do.” But from this struggle came purpose.

But from this struggle came purpose.

With the guidance and compassion of a dedicated hospice nurse Joline Murillo who became family, and through lived experience, advocacy, and spiritual growth, a vision was born: to create a better, more compassionate support system for families walking similar paths.

I have since become a certified Reiki practitioner (White Light Reiki and Usui Reiki Ryoho), an End-of-Life Doula, and trained in Sibshops, workshops supporting siblings of children with special needs. Alongside my son Rocco, I continue to serve the community through volunteering and connecting with nature as a source of healing, and transforming pain into purpose.

As Mother Teresa once reflected, “I know God will not give me anything I can’t handle. Sometimes I just wish that He, didn’t trust me so much.” This sentiment echoes the reality of our journey, one of strength, surrender, and faith through profound loss.
