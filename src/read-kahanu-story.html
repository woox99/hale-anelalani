Kahanu Ohana's Story

My name is Tracy, I have 2 sons who both have /had Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy (DMD), which is a muscle wasting disease. There is no cure, only treatments to help slow the progress.

I knew there was a possibility that if I had boys, that they might have DMD. We had family history with Duchenne.

When I found out I was pregnant with My Punuali’i, I decided to get a genetic test to find out if he had Duchenne. The test came back positive,so of course the Doctor gave me options, but after I heard his heart beating, my decision was already made to keep him. I knew I already loved him. I knew my journey for my family would be a challenge and test our love and strength.

So when I became pregnant, with my Pulikikaua,my second son, at first we thought he might be a girl, but the second ultrasound proved he was a boy. So we did the genetic test for him and he also had Duchenne. I was given the same options as with the brother and I wanted him too. So my journey was not going to be an easy one, with hardly any family support. My partner in life Earl and I decided we would be there for each other, even though we had a rocky start and many challenges to our relationship ,we both stuck it out. Even when our Punuali’i left this earth, at the age of 19 years, December 27,2022.

We still have our youngest Pulikikaua with us, he is now 21. Our goal is to make sure he is comfortable, loved, safe and cared for everyday, always. I would move mountains for him, if there could be a cure, one day or even if that doesn’t happen in his lifetime, for other boys with Duchenne.

With all honesty, I never regretted a day of getting to know my beautiful boys. They taught me not to take life and everyday for granted. After my Punuali’i passed, he taught me many lessons, especially to never give up and to live the life he couldn’t.

Having a place like Hale ʻĀnelalani would mean so much to families like ours. A place where I could take a moment to breathe and also take care of our own health while knowing my son is being cared for with love and compassion. A place where we could just be mom, dad, and son, instead of always being caregivers. I wish we had a place like this when my Punualiʻi was still here with us. Somewhere we could have made more memories together, found comfort, and felt less alone on our journey. I know firsthand how exhausting and heartbreaking this journey can be, but also how beautiful and precious every moment is. My hope is that Hale ʻĀnelalani will be able to provide families of Hawai'i with the support, love, and peace they deserve, because sometimes even the strongest ʻohana needs someone to care for them, too.
